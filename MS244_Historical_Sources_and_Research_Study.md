# Wellcome Collection MS.244 — Historical Sources, Provenance & Research Study

**Shelfmark:** Wellcome Collection MS.244 (Accession No. `44190`)  
**Work ID:** `ae3q6kcm` · **Sierra ID:** `b19608652`  
**Compilers & Contributors:** Henry Dineley / Dingley (`fl. 1564–1633`) of Hanley Castle, Worcestershire; Francis Dineley (`1591/1592`); Dr. John Sadler (`1615–1664`); and an earlier Tudor heraldic/calendar scribe  
**Date Span:** `1564–1652+` (core compilation `1591–1633`, incorporating earlier 1564 leaves and mid-17th-century additions)

---

## 1. Codicological & Physical Structure

Wellcome MS.244 is a large folio volume (`41.5 × 27 cm`) bound in its original blind-stamped calfskin over wooden boards with brass corner pieces and central diamond bosses (Canvases 1, 436–438). Digitised across **444 IIIF canvases**, the codex comprises four distinct structural units:

1. **Preliminary Leaves (`Canvases 7–38`, Prelim. Fol. 1r–16v):** Unpaginated (and partially foliated `5`, `15`, `16`) leaves containing English law terms, angling and rod-making instructions, horizontal/vertical sundials and an astronomical quadrant, a **Roman Catholic liturgical calendar** in red and black paired with the **Four Seasons medical regimens** (*Secreta Secretorum* / *Regimen Sanitatis* tradition), **1564 apothecary weights and measures**, the **1613 Dineley coat of arms**, perpetual Easter and Solar Cycle (`1645–1668`) tables, Leonard Fioravanti and Anselm Boetius extracts, and technical drawings of a surgical pipe (*"A Pype to heale thrustes of my owen invencion"*).
2. **Main Paginated Medical & Surgical Compendium (`Canvases 39–382`, `pp. 2–353`):** Double-column red-ruled folio pages systematically organised by ailment and therapeutic class — opening with *Repercussives* and *Refrigeratives*, moving through ulcers, fistulas, wounds, *Morbus Gallicus* (pox), gout, scrofula, dropsy, palsy, hernia/rupture, dysentery, gynaecology, *Restauratives*, jaundice, spleen, stone, colic, stomach, facial skin, burns, epilepsy, quinsy, cough/phthisis, ears, gangrene, pleurisy, and concluding with veterinary and compound plasters.
3. **Reversed Endleaves & Weather Chronicles (`Canvases 383–387`):** Inscribed from the back of the volume, preserving Henry Dineley's meteorological and agricultural notes for **1564** (*"the coldest sprynge and the wyndyst that ever I h:d: dyd see"*), **1592** (the killing of bay trees across England), and **1598** (*"no fruite, Apples nor peares"*), alongside the **3 December 1591** gift inscription from Francis Dineley.
4. **Armorial & Genealogical Pedigrees (`Canvases 388–430`):** Partly emblazoned 5×6 heraldic shield grids (letters A–B) interleaved with large circular genealogical pedigree diagrams for the **FitzAlan (Earls of Arundel)**, **Percy (Earls of Northumberland)**, and **Talbot (Earls of Shrewsbury and Grafton, dated 1601)**.

## 2. Authorship, Family Network & Empirical Marks (`H.D.` / `☾ ☾`)

The manuscript's primary compiler is **Henry Dineley (or Dingley / Dyneley)** of **Hanley Castle, Worcestershire**. On the reversed endleaf (Canvas 385), an inscription in the older hand records:

> *"Liber henrici Dineley ex dono ffrancisci Dineley armigeri. Anno 1591 3 decembris."*  
> (*"The book of Henry Dineley, by the gift of Francis Dineley, esquire, 3 December 1591."*)

Beneath it appear Henry's own ownership declarations (*"henry Dyneley of hanley Chastell oweth this Booke. Per me Henry Dyneley"*, *"que serra serra qd henry Dineley"*, *"Rien sans trauayle"*). Throughout the volume, Dineley weaves a rich web of family and regional memory:
- **Generational & Kinship Ties:** References to *"my grandfather, my father and myself at Tewkesbury"* (`p. 339`), *"my father anno 1548"* (`p. 335`), *"my great grandfather Wm D."* (`p. 199`), his maternal grandmother **Elizabeth Ergas** (`pp. 126, 130, 163`), and **Elizabeth Dingley** (`p. 100`).
- **West Midlands & Welsh Marches Geography:** Clinical cases and receipts are anchored in **Hanley Castle**, **Tewkesbury**, **Evesham** (`2 April 1633`), **Church Lawford** (Warwickshire), **Bristol**, **Wrexton** (`Dec 1625`), and **London** (for specialised apothecary wares and blue-black pin paper).
- **Empirical Verification Marks (`H.D.` and `☾ ☾`):** On Prelim. Fol. 16v (Canvas 38), Dineley explains his personal marginalia system: *"These ij markes ☾ ℞ shewe yowe qt the medecyne [marked] by mee: and one marke by sum other ☾"*, and at the end of the compendium he attests that wherever his character `HD` stands, *"that have I Henry dineley prouyd withowte dowte and no other have I myself prouyd"*. Clinical case notes span **1608 to 1633**, including candid accounts of both successes and failures (e.g., *"Tried this but yt failed"* on `p. 207` regarding the 1627 Isle of Rhé toad remedy).

## 3. Confessional Identity & Later 17th-Century Strata

The preliminary **Roman Catholic Calendar** (Canvases 9–12) retains uncancelled pre-Reformation feasts including **St. Thomas of Canterbury** (`29 December`) and **Corpus Christi**, signalling a Catholic/recusant milieu consistent with Worcestershire gentry families of the late Tudor period.

After 1633, the volume passed into the hands of **Dr. John Sadler (1615–1664)**, whose crisp italic Latin prescriptions (signed `D. Sadler` or `S`) appear throughout the margins and lower columns (e.g., `pp. 66, 100, 181, 187, 193, 203, 207, 210, 217, 240, 280`). Further mid-17th-century additions cite **Nicholas Culpeper's *London Dispensatory*** (first published `1649`, cited as `Lond: Disp: Culp:` on `p. 201`), include a **1645** rupture plaster (`p. 166`), a **1652** comfrey receipt (`p. 162`), and a **1645–1668 Solar Cycle Table** (Canvas 30).

## 4. Medical, Surgical & Iatrochemical Sources

MS.244 bridges medieval English surgery, Renaissance humanism, and Paracelsian iatrochemistry:
- **English Surgical Tradition:** Repeated citations of **John Arderne** (`fl. 1370`) and **John Woodall** (*The Surgions Mate*, `p. 188`), alongside detailed practical illustrations of surgical **tents**, **harelip sutures**, **dry-stitching plasters**, **trusses** (`p. 170`), and Dineley's own invented **surgical pipe** (`p. 71`, Prelim. 16v).
- **Continental Iatrochemists & Botanists:** **Leonardo Fioravanti** (*Petra Philosophale*, *Phiorovante Balme*, *Unguento Magno*), **Paracelsus** (*Stipticum Paracelsi*), **Joseph Quercetanus**, **Anselm Boetius** (*De Gemmis et Lapidibus*), **Rembert Dodoens**, **Pietro Andrea Mattioli**, and **Nicolás Monardes / Clusius** (on *Tacamahaca* and *Sarsaparilla*).
- **Angling, Horology & Heraldry:** Beyond medicine, the volume is a rare witness to Tudor gentlemanly pursuits — preserving detailed instructions for making **carp baits and jointed fishing rods** with tin/brass ferrules (Prelim. 15r–v), **sundial construction**, and **late Elizabethan heraldic genealogy**.
