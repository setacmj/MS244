## Canvas 127 — 89

[![Canvas 127](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0127.JP2/full/600,/0/default.jpg)](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0127.JP2/full/1400,/0/default.jpg)

*Page 89 (also numbered 'lxxxix' at bottom left): Two columns of receipts for inflammation, erysipelas ('Hellfier Erisipula'), and cooling waters/oils. Left column includes Latin cures for erysipelas of the shins, frogspawn water ('Spermatis Ranarum'), Mr. Arden's 'Salue populi Rosing' (cooling salve), coralline ointment for hot tumours, St. Anthony's fire, and stramonia water/ointment. Right column includes a lime-and-calamine wash ('Ad Eresipulas', with cross-reference to p. 67), egg-white dressing for green wounds, 'A coling water', 'A coolinge oyle', and 'An Exelent coling water' (persicaria and tragopogon / goat's beard, referencing pp. 244-245).*

## Canvas 128 — 90

[![Canvas 128](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0128.JP2/full/600,/0/default.jpg)](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0128.JP2/full/1400,/0/default.jpg)

*Page 90 (also numbered 'xc' at bottom center): Two columns on inflammation, erysipelas, and imposthumes. Left column opens with Mr. John Arderne's cure of a woman in Newark using asphodel roots, followed by 'ffor the Erysipelas & Inflammation' (referencing p. 345), 'ffor heate whether it pimples or Scabbes', 'Ad pedes tumorosos inflatos' (linden bark), 'Oleum Stramonie for scabbes' (attributed to Mr. Bart[let]), and a Latin treatise 'De Apostomatibus' classifying phlegmon, axima, erysipelas, scirrhus, cancer, fistula, and malum mortuum. Right column gives cabbage and earthworm receipts for ripening and drawing imposthumes, internal imposthume drinks (scabious, turmeric, celandine, figs), and 'Contra dolorem Apostomatis'.*

## Canvas 146 — 107

[![Canvas 146](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0146.JP2/full/600,/0/default.jpg)](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0146.JP2/full/1400,/0/default.jpg)

*Page 107 (numbered '107' at top left, Roman signature 'Cvij' at foot; running header 'Of the yearde'): Surgical and medical receipts in Latin and English for inflammation, canker, ulcers, excoriation, and burns of the penis, including Henry Dineley's dated 1617 personal case note on treating a burn with frog-spawn water, Unguentum regium, and Flos unguentorum, instructions for rolling small surgical tents, a receipt from Leonardo Fioravanti for superfluous flesh, and directions for making tents from a dried ox's yard.*
