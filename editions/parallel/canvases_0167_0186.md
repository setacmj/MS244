## Canvas 167 — 129

[![Canvas 167](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0167.JP2/full/600,/0/default.jpg)](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0167.JP2/full/1400,/0/default.jpg)

*Page 129 recto (numbered '129' at top right, Roman signature 'Cxxjx' at foot; running headers 'Podagra' and 'the Gowte'): Principally a Latin treatise on the humoral causes and cures of gout (hot, cold, sanguine, and choleric Podagra), including phlebotomy, mulsam, sea-water fomentation, fox-fat and badger-fat cataplasms, horse-bone marrow ointment, and purgative pills; followed in English by methods to drive the gout downward or upward with stonecrop and woodbine, and Dr. Carpenter's gout plaster ('ffor the Goute Doct[or] Carp[en]t[e]r').*

*Page 130 verso (numbered '130' at top left, Roman signature 'Cxxx' at foot; running headers 'Podagra' and 'the Gowte'): English and Latin receipts for gout, including elderberry and wallwort water ('A Noble medicyne for ye goute'), urine-based herb ointments for cold gout and ache, 'Contra omnem guttam' (bacon-lard and juniper baked in an earthen pot), 'Oleum ad Guttam' (henbane seed oil), Grandmother Elizabeth Ergas's herb-christopher receipt ('my Gra[n]dmother El. Ergas'), Mother de Brantford's sheep-suet gout plaster, broom-flower ointment, lead/litharge swelling plaster, and cheese-curd/milk-curds with bull's gall and badger's grease (with a cross-reference to p. 126).*
