## Canvas 107 — 69

[![Canvas 107](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0107.JP2/full/600,/0/default.jpg)](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0107.JP2/full/1400,/0/default.jpg)

*Page 69 (Recto): 'Conglutinatives'. Opens across the top with 'Tewe Balmes like one the other almost in althinge', followed by two parallel columns comparing 'The Excellent oyle off Aparitio' (left) and 'The Ladye Cromwells Her Balme' (right, with cross-references to Oleum Lucatelli on pp. 133 and 347 and a green balm). The lower section spans full-width with instructions 'To dresse woundes w[i]t[h] Aparitio' and 'To Skin a Soore w[i]t[h] Aparitio', concluding with a provenance note recording that a Spanish lady taught the receipt to an English knight ('HW.'). Marked '69' at top right and 'lxix' at bottom.*

## Canvas 108 — 70

*Page 70 (Verso): Two-column collection of Latin and English balms and wound oils. Left column contains 'Oleum Balsami' (in Latin, with English gloss 'cacabo terreo is a pott of erthe' and note on colouring with Sanguis Veneris), 'Balsamus ad uulnera' (distilled balsam for deep ulcers, noli-me-tangere, and palsy), and 'A greene Balme'. Right column contains 'A preciouse Balme', 'A precious wound oyle' (Hypericum flowers steeped in the sun), 'ffor Cutts and soores' (oil of turpentine, wax, and rosemary), 'Oyle off Balme for wounds' (balm leaves in balneum Mariae and sun), 'Chimicor[um] Balsamu[m]' (sublimed flowers of sulphur and camphor distilled by alembic), and a note on colouring oils red with alkanet. Marked '70' at top left and 'lxx' at bottom.*

## Canvas 109 — 71

[![Canvas 109](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0109.JP2/full/600,/0/default.jpg)](https://iiif.wellcomecollection.org/image/b19608652_MS_244_0109.JP2/full/1400,/0/default.jpg)

*Page 71 (Recto): Two-column surgical treatise on 'Sanguis ueneris' (alkanet-coloured wound oil). Left column gives the base receipt (oil of roses or salad oil boiled with alkanet root), substitutions (turpentine oil for head wounds, May butter, capon/goose/hen/badger grease), and a detailed personal account of treating deep narrow thrust wounds (rapier, pike, poniard, tuck) using a small non-metallic glyster-pipe (illustrated with a pen-and-ink drawing of a tapered pipe/syringe). Right column explains why he abandoned metal syringes (which scald the sore), criticises surgeons who fail to keep the oil hot, praises God for the blessing of alkanet, and gives dressing instructions for stitched and unstitched wounds ('statu quo prius'). Marked '71' at top right and 'lxxi' at bottom.*
